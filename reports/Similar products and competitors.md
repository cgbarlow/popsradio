# Nothing does what Pop's Radio does, but its neighbours are close

Nov 16, 2026 · @Chris Barlow

## Summary

**No product found does all four things Pop's Radio does:** one button with no screen, walkie-talkie messages rather than calls, the whole family on one channel from radios and phones, and no subscription. But each part exists somewhere, and four kinds of product are near neighbours:

- **Screen-free companions for older people.** [Ato](https://heyato.ai) is the closest: a radio-style device with a button and a knob, voice notes to and from a family app, US$179, with a free tier and an optional US$249-a-year membership. Its next batch ships in February 2027.
- **Simple video devices for older people.** Komp, GrandPad, Konnekt and New Zealand's Kitcal all make calling easy, and all charge monthly or quote on request.
- **Push-to-talk radios on the mobile network.** They give town-to-town walkie-talkie range today, but are built for work crews, and their "no monthly fee" usually means a yearly fee or an unverifiable SIM.
- **Screen-free home phones for kids.** Tin Can, Pinwheel Home, Ooma MyPhone and Wiley. This is a crowded, fast-moving market, and it is where the kids' mini phone would compete.

**What only Pop's Radio offers:** walkie-talkie, not a call, so nobody has to answer; radios and family phones on the same channel; channels paid for life with no subscription; and open designs you can build yourself.

**What it lacks against them:** no video (Komp, Konnekt), no voice assistant (Ato), and it needs home Wi-Fi where push-to-talk radios and some tablets carry their own mobile data.

**The biggest market risk is trust in "no subscription".** A push-to-talk dealer argues that "lifetime free" service does not exist, and buyers have reason to agree. The prepaid fund has to be explained plainly on the box.

New Zealand market size and local substitutes (senior phones, Spacetalk, medical alarms, UHF radios) are covered in the [NZ addressable market report](NZ%20addressable%20market%20and%20opportunities.md). This report looks wider, at who else is solving the same problem.

## What the market says

**Subscriptions are the norm.** Every comparable device with a service charges for it, from NZ$19.99 a month (Tin Can) to £19 (Komp) and US$25 to 65 (GrandPad). "No subscription, ever" is a genuine difference, but only if people believe it.

**"Free for life" has a bad name.** PeakPTT's argument against cheap radios is the same one buyers will raise: servers cost money every year, so who pays? The answer has to be on the box: running a channel costs a dollar or two a year, that is prepaid into a ring-fenced fund, and the code is published so the radio can outlive us.

**Free tier plus paid extras works.** Ato pairs a free tier with an optional membership, and Tin Can and Pinwheel make calls between their own devices free and charge for calling out. That matches the plan: radios free to run, the family app paid.

**The founder story is common.** Ato and Yayagram both started as one grandchild's build for one grandparent. It sells, but it is not unique.

**Kids' devices move fast and fight on price.** Tin Can launched a pre-order in NZ, and Ooma is paying families to swap Tin Cans for its phone. Relay's exit shows how hard the consumer kids' market is. The kids' mini phone should stay "later", and compete on walkie-talkie and no subscription rather than price.

**Care money exists.** Konnekt tells buyers they may be eligible for government funding, which echoes the medical-alarm precedent in the NZ market report.

## What to do with this

1. **Lead with what is unique:** one button, no answering, the whole family on one channel, no subscription.
2. **Explain the prepaid fund in one sentence** wherever the price appears, and publish it.
3. **Watch Ato closely.** It reaches the same buyers with a similar device and a family app, and it is shipping. If it adds a walkie-talkie mode it becomes a direct competitor.
4. **Do not compete with video or AI.** Point families who need video at Komp or Konnekt; Pop's Radio is for the moments in between.
5. **For the kids' phone, plan against Tin Can, Pinwheel and Ooma,** not against toy walkie-talkies.

## Gaps

- No NZ availability was checked for Ato, Komp, GrandPad, Pinwheel, Ooma or Wiley.
- Kitcal's price and Konnekt's NZ price were not published.
- The Amazon "lifetime free" radios were not tested, and the claim against them comes from a competing seller.
- Sales figures beyond those the companies publish (Komp 14,000 families, Ato 10,000 family members) were not found.
