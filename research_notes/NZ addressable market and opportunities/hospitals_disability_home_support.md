# NZ hospitals, hospices, disability support and home-and-community support: B2B/government markets and funding pathways for an assistive communication device

Research date: 15 November 2026. Method note: in this session, direct page fetches to government domains (health.govt.nz, msd.govt.nz) and to Hospice NZ's PDF host (nationbuilder) were blocked by the session network, so every finding below comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from reading them in full. Figures should be checked against the primary documents before anyone relies on them in a pitch or a business case. Where a figure comes only from my background knowledge, it is in Gaps or Inferences and labelled as unverified.

## 1. Public hospitals: count, beds by region, older persons' health/AT&R, mental health, length of stay, patient Wi-Fi

### Takeaway
Health NZ runs 86 hospital sites. I found no consolidated, current national or regional bed table in public sources; the bed data that does exist is per facility, either in Ministry of Health certification listings or in large-hospital figures. Older persons' mental health inpatient units are small, roughly 10-19 beds each. Free patient Wi-Fi is common but was rolled out site by site under the old DHBs, and several sites use a login or splash page (for example, login by NHI number in Wellington). That matters directly, because the device cannot get past a captive portal.

### Inferences
- Wi-Fi with an NHI login or splash page (Wellington/Hutt, and probably others) would block the device as designed. Hospital deployments would likely need one of three things: Health NZ IT allowlisting the device's MAC address on the patient or guest network, a dedicated pre-shared-key SSID, or a cellular/4G fallback. This needs checking site by site.
- Because Wi-Fi arrangements are still on DHB-era contracts, they vary by district. Pilot sites should be picked by Wi-Fi type: open SSIDs such as Counties Manukau "Hospital Hotspot" look most promising.
- The patients who would benefit most (AT&R/older persons' health, mental health, spinal units) sit in relatively small units of 10-30 beds each. A national hospital-ward market is plausibly in the low thousands of beds, not tens of thousands. That is my estimate, not a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No national or by-region (Northern, Te Manawa Taki, Central, Te Waipounamu) total of public beds was found. Best primary sources to try: the Ministry of Health "Legal Entity Summary" / certified-providers listing for public hospitals, Health NZ annual report 2024/25, or an OIA request.
- No national counts were found for AT&R/older persons' health rehab beds, total adult mental health inpatient beds, or spinal unit beds. The Auckland Spinal Rehabilitation Unit (Ōtara) and the Burwood Spinal Unit (Christchurch) are the two spinal units from background knowledge; their bed counts are unverified.
- Average length of stay for long-stay patients (AT&R, mental health): no source found.
- Whether Health NZ has standardised patient Wi-Fi since 2022, or which sites use captive portals today: no source found.

## 2. Private and surgical hospitals, and hospices

### Takeaway
Southern Cross Healthcare is the largest private surgical operator: 10 wholly owned hospitals plus 7 joint ventures, and more than 118,000 patients in FY2025. Surgical stays are short, so the fit with this product is weak. Hospices are a better fit thematically. Hospice NZ has 32 independent charity members, which reached 20,181 people in 2024/25. Fewer than half of hospices have inpatient units, usually of 6-10 beds, and hospices are under severe funding pressure, cutting beds in 2025-26.

- A MartinJenkins report (2025) for Hospice NZ quantified the value of hospices to the health system and flagged significant cost pressure. — [Hospice NZ](https://www.hospice.org.nz/hospices_under_significant_cost_pressure_a_landmark_report_shows_the_real_value_of_hospices_to_health_system); [Exec summary (Nelson Hospice)](https://www.nelsonhospice.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/sites/329/2025/04/Executive_Summary_MartinJenkinsReport.pdf)

### Inferences
- Most of the roughly 20,000 hospice patients a year are cared for at home in the community, not in inpatient beds. Hospices could therefore lend radios to home-based patients and families, for example a patient-to-family link. Given the funding squeeze, purchases would more likely be funded by donors or bequests, or done as a "hospice shop" retail partnership, than paid from operating budgets.
- National inpatient hospice beds are probably in the order of 100-150, if about 13 units have 6-10 beds each. This is an estimate from the 2022 survey, and recent cuts may have lowered it.
- Private surgical hospitals have short stays, typically 1-3 nights, so they are a poor fit. Private rehab or long-stay providers would be a better target, but none were researched.

### Gaps
- No national hospice inpatient bed total, regional breakdown of hospices, or patients by region was found; the Hospice NZ annual report PDF could not be read directly.
- Total private hospital beds nationally (all operators, including Mercy, Wakefield/Evolution, Boulcott and others) were not found.

## 3. Disability support (DSS within MSD): people, residential care, providers

### Takeaway
Disability Support Services has sat within MSD since 2024. Its 2025/26 appropriation is about NZ$2.57b, and its ongoing baseline is cited as about NZ$2.77b a year. At 31 December 2024, 7,293 people were in DSS-funded residential care, 6,154 of them in community group homes. Group-home pricing is being moved to banded rates across four regions. IDEA Services (IHC) is the largest provider, supporting more than 3,000 people with intellectual disabilities.

### Cited Findings
- **7,293 people** were supported across the four DSS-funded residential care services at **31 December 2024**, of whom **6,154 were in community group homes**. — [MSD Cabinet paper: Stabilising DSS-funded residential care (2025)](https://www.msd.govt.nz/documents/about-msd-and-our-work/publications-resources/information-releases/cabinet-papers/2025/stabilising-disability-support-services-funded-residential-care/paper-stabilising-disability-support-services-funded-residential-care.pdf)
- Group homes were funded through **2,920 different prices**, and 3,346 people (54%) were on 112 contracted rates. Cabinet agreed a **banded-rate model with six bands in each of four regions**. — [MSD Cabinet paper](https://www.msd.govt.nz/documents/about-msd-and-our-work/publications-resources/information-releases/cabinet-papers/2025/stabilising-disability-support-services-funded-residential-care/paper-stabilising-disability-support-services-funded-residential-care.pdf); [Cabinet minute SOU-25-MIN-0015](https://www.msd.govt.nz/documents/about-msd-and-our-work/publications-resources/information-releases/cabinet-papers/2025/stabilising-disability-support-services-funded-residential-care/sou-25-min-0015-minute.pdf)
- Budget 2025 gave **NZ$240m over the forecast period (NZ$60m a year ongoing)** for residential care, and **NZ$1b over four years** for DSS cost pressures and access. — [MSD Budget 2025 factsheet](https://www.msd.govt.nz/about-msd-and-our-work/newsroom/budget/2025/factsheets/disability-support-services-cost-pressures-supporting-disabled-people.html); [DSS Budget 2025 summary](https://www.disabilitysupport.govt.nz/about-us/news/budget-2025-summary-of-dss-budget-funding)
- DSS total appropriation for **2025/26 is NZ$2.57b**; the ongoing baseline after recent Budgets is cited as **NZ$2.77b a year**. The sources differ slightly. — [DSS: Budgets](https://www.disabilitysupport.govt.nz/about-us/budgets)
- **IDEA Services** (part of the IHC group) is "New Zealand's largest provider of services for more than 3,000 people with intellectual disabilities". It offers residential services with 24/7 support as well as supported living. — [IHC: IDEA Services](https://ihc.org.nz/idea-services); [IHC careers](https://careers.ihc.org.nz/about/idea-services)
- **Emerge Aotearoa** provides specialised accommodation and residential services for people with intellectual disabilities, including RIDSAS (secure/compulsory care) in Christchurch and the Upper South Island. — [Emerge Aotearoa: Disability support](https://emergeaotearoa.org.nz/our-services/mental-health-disability/disability-support/)
- The HCHA provider directory lists IDEA Services and other disability and home-support providers by region. — [HCHA Find a provider](https://www.hcha.org.nz/find-a-provider/providers)

### Inferences
- About 6,150 people live in community group homes, plausibly 1,000-1,500 homes at 4-6 residents each (an estimate, not sourced). A radio per resident, or per house linked to family and coordinators, is a concrete B2B unit. Buyers would be provider organisations such as IDEA/IHC, Spectrum Care, Emerge and Geneva, buying from their own operating or discretionary budgets, since DSS funds residential care as a bundled rate.
- Because the banded rates mean providers are under margin pressure, low-cost or consumables-style pricing (NZ$2-5 a month) would be easier to justify than capital purchases.

### Gaps
- Total number of people receiving DSS-funded support, all types: not found in this session. From background knowledge it is roughly 40,000-50,000 people allocated support; this is unverified and should be checked against the DSS "Disability Support System data" / MSD annual report 2025.
- Number of people in supported living, and residential or supported-living counts by region: not found.
- Headcounts for Spectrum Care, Geneva and Emerge: not found.

## 4. Home and community support services (HCSS) for older people

### Takeaway
About 72,000 older people received Health NZ-funded in-home care at some point in 2024/25, with about 48,000 receiving it at any one time. The sector is a few dozen providers, about 70-75 in DHB-era counts, and Health NZ is consolidating contracts: in 2026 three Auckland providers serving about 4,500 people were not renewed.

### Cited Findings
- **~72,000 older people received in-home care for some or all of 2024/25; ~48,000 at any one time.** — [Ministry of Health: Aged Care Ministerial Advisory Group, key facts & figures (Aug 2026)](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2026-08/aged-care-ministerial-advisory-group-report-key-facts-figures.pdf). Older estimates of 75,000-80,000 use different counting methods. — [Treasury/Productivity Commission Appendix E (2024)](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2024-05/pc-inq-mess-appendix-e-home-based-support-for-older-people.pdf)
- About **75 HCSS providers** contracted to DHBs, ACC and disability support (2020); about **70 NGOs** per a 2024 Treasury appendix (DHB-era data). — [MoH National Framework for HCSS (2020)](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2020-06/national_framework_for_home_and_community_support_services_29sept2020.pdf); [Treasury Appendix E](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2024-05/pc-inq-mess-appendix-e-home-based-support-for-older-people.pdf)
- In August 2026, Health NZ decided **not to renew contracts with three providers** covering Auckland, Counties Manukau and Waitematā. Those providers support about **4,500 older and disabled people**, and **more than 1,200 home-support jobs** were put at risk. — [Enliven Northern (Presbyterian Support)](https://www.enlivennorthern.org.nz/about-us/news/healthnz-decision-puts-more-than-1200-home-support-workers-jobs-at-risk); [Community Scoop, Aug 2026](https://community.scoop.co.nz/2026/08/health-nz-decision-puts-more-than-1200-home-support-workers-jobs-at-risk/)
- A current national HCSS Tier 2 service specification exists, dated August 2025. — [Health NZ HCSS Tier 2 spec (2025-08)](https://static.info.content.health.nz/docs/health-pros/topics/nationwide-service-framework/ch/CH_T2_HomeAndCommunitySupportServices_202508.pdf)
- Major providers listed on Eldernet include HealthCare NZ and Geneva Healthcare; HCHA keeps a member directory by region. — [Eldernet: HealthCare NZ](https://www.eldernet.co.nz/provider/Healthcare-NZ/home-help-and-personal-care); [Eldernet: Geneva](https://www.eldernet.co.nz/provider/geneva-healthcare/home-help-and-personal-care); [HCHA](https://www.hcha.org.nz/find-a-provider/providers)

### Inferences
- Of the roughly 48,000 concurrent home-support clients, many live alone. A client-to-coordinator or client-to-family radio fits, but provider margins are thin, and the August 2026 Auckland contract consolidation shows Health NZ is pushing toward fewer, larger providers. The best route is probably a pilot with one of the large consolidated providers, where the radio is pitched as reducing missed-visit and scheduling phone calls.
- No regional breakdown was found. Pro-rating by the over-65 population would put roughly 35-38% in the Northern region; that is an estimate only.

### Gaps
- No regional client counts, client counts per provider (Access, HealthCare NZ, Care on Call, Presbyterian Support/Enliven, Geneva), or current provider count were found. The figure of "over 30,000 New Zealanders" seen in search is self-reported by one provider and could not be attributed with confidence.

## 5. Funding pathways for an assistive communication device

### Takeaway
The realistic public pathway for an individual is EMS, funded by DSS/MSD and delivered by Enable NZ (most of the North Island south of the Bombay Hills, plus the whole South Island) and Accessable (Auckland/Northland). EMS explicitly includes "communication devices", but only for disabilities expected to last six months or more, only on request from a credentialled EMS assessor, as loaned equipment, and with funds rationed. The MSD Disability Allowance pays for medical alarms only from accredited suppliers. For organisations, the Office for Seniors Age-friendly Fund (NZ$5k-20k, "social connection, digital confidence") is the closest-fitting grant, but the 2026 round closed on 30 September 2026.

### Cited Findings
- EMS eligibility: a disability likely to last **at least six months**; a need for specific equipment **identified by an EMS assessor**; a need not already covered by ACC; NZ residency. **Only a credentialled EMS assessor can request funded equipment.** — [DSS: Equipment and aids](https://www.disabilitysupport.govt.nz/disabled-people/support-and-services/equipment-and-aids); [Enable NZ: DSS-funded equipment](https://www.enable.co.nz/about-us/what-we-do/equipment-for-disabled-people/government-funded)
- EMS is funded by DSS, which has been within MSD since **October 2024**. Enable NZ and Accessable are the contracted providers that supply and manage equipment. Equipment is **owned by DSS and lent free for as long as needed**. — [Enable NZ](https://www.enable.co.nz/about-us/what-we-do/equipment-for-disabled-people/government-funded)
- Enable covers the North Island south of Meremere (Bombay Hills), the South Island, Stewart Island and the Chatham Islands; Accessable covers the rest (Auckland/Northland). — [Enable NZ](https://www.enable.co.nz/about-us/what-we-do/equipment-for-disabled-people/government-funded)
- The EMS-funded list includes **communication devices** and visual or vibrating alert systems. The EMS Equipment Manual defines a communication device as equipment "to enable a person to communicate independently and reliably". — [Enable NZ](https://www.enable.co.nz/about-us/what-we-do/equipment-for-disabled-people/government-funded); [EMS Equipment Manual (Oct 2014)](https://www.whaikaha.govt.nz/assets/EMS-Service-Provider-Documents/Equipment-manual-V2.pdf)
- Funding is rationed: the Ministry "is not able to provide funding for equipment and modifications to meet all of the needs identified". EMS protocols and service specifications (2014-15, MoH era) govern assessors and suppliers. — [EMS Service Specification (Feb 2015)](https://www.disabilitysupport.govt.nz/assets/Equipment-and-Modification-Services1.pdf); [Therapy and AT/Equipment protocols](https://www.whaikaha.govt.nz/assets/EMS-Service-Provider-Documents/Therapy-and-Assistive-Technology-Equipment-Protocols-v2.pdf)
- MSD Disability Allowance can include a medical alarm's cost if the alarm is needed ongoing because of a disability, is supported by a health practitioner, and is **supplied by an MSD-accredited company**. — [Work and Income: Medical alarms](https://www.workandincome.govt.nz/eligibility/health-and-disability/medical-alarms.html)
- Office for Seniors **Age-friendly Fund**: grants of **NZ$5,000-20,000** (2025 guidance: NZ$5k-15k excl. GST) for councils, community organisations and non-profits. Focus areas include "social connection, digital confidence, community participation". The 2026 round opened on 7 August and **closed on 30 September 2026**. — [Office for Seniors: Apply](https://www.officeforseniors.govt.nz/our-work/age-friendly-communities/apply-for-an-age-friendly-community-grant); [Grants now available (2026)](https://www.officeforseniors.govt.nz/latest-news/grants-now-available-for-community-projects-that-support-older-people); [2025 guidance PDF](https://www.officeforseniors.govt.nz/assets/documents/our-work/age-friendly/Age-friendly-fund/2025-Age-friendly-Fund-Guidance.pdf)
- Office for Seniors Digital Literacy Training for Seniors: Budget 2022 provided **NZ$1.341m over three years**. It is a training programme, not a device fund. — [Office for Seniors: Digital literacy](https://www.officeforseniors.govt.nz/our-work/digital-inclusion/digital-literacy-training-for-seniors); [Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/more-digital-training-seniors-rolls-out-across-new-zealand)
- A secondary blog summarises NZ digital inclusion grants that cover access, devices and digital literacy. — [Tahua blog](https://www.tahua.io/blog/2d30613b317b/digital-inclusion-grants-nz)

### Inferences
- To get onto EMS, the device would need to be in Enable/Accessable's supply catalogue, which means becoming a supplier to them, and assessors (OTs, speech-language therapists) would need to justify it against a disability-related communication need. An everyday "keep in touch" use is unlikely to meet the EMS bar. A framing for people with cognitive, vision or dexterity impairments who cannot use phones could. The low price (NZ$50-125) falls below many low-cost-equipment thresholds; EMS has a low-cost equipment pathway in its protocols, but its current threshold is unverified.
- Disability Allowance currently covers only accredited medical alarm suppliers. The radio is "not a medical alarm", so it does not qualify. A separate, individual Disability Allowance claim for ongoing disability costs, such as a monthly channel fee, might be possible with health practitioner support; this is unverified.
- The Age-friendly Fund fits a community pilot well, run by an Age Concern or a council with the manufacturer as supplier, but the next round would be around August 2027.

## 6. Health NZ procurement (GETS, AoG panels, Pharmac devices), timelines, and precedents

### Takeaway
Hospital medical device procurement is split: Pharmac leads 27 device categories and Health NZ leads 28, with Health NZ keeping the budget. Pharmac's national contracts cover about 70% of devices, roughly NZ$607m a year. A consumer comms gadget would almost certainly not count as a "hospital medical device". It would more likely be bought as general goods by individual hospitals or wards, through charitable or hospital foundation funds, or via GETS for larger volumes. I found no sourced precedents for patient-communication devices or COVID-era iPad donations in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Government split device procurement: **Pharmac leads 27 categories, Health NZ 28**; Health NZ keeps the medical device budget and resources Pharmac for Health Technology Assessments. — [Pharmac: Future of national medical device procurement](https://www.pharmac.govt.nz/news-and-resources/news/future-of-national-medical-device-procurement); [MoH: Medical device procurement](https://www.health.govt.nz/strategies-initiatives/programmes-and-initiatives/medical-device-procurement)
- Pharmac national contracts cover **about 70% of medical devices used by Health NZ, about NZ$607m a year** of hospital spend (June 2024). — [HiNZ, Jun 2024](https://www.hinz.org.nz/news/676072/Pharmac-contracts-70-percent-of-medical-devices.htm)
- Health NZ hospitals buying devices on Pharmac's Hospital Medical Devices List must use the national contract terms where available, but may still choose other suppliers. Example: an Obex Medical listing agreement for feeding equipment, effective 1 December 2025. — [Pharmac: Hospital Medical Devices List](https://www.pharmac.govt.nz/hospital-devices/devices-list); [Pharmac: Obex decision](https://www.pharmac.govt.nz/news-and-resources/consultations-and-decisions/decision-to-approve-agreement-for-medical-devices-supplied-by-obex-medical-limited)
- Pharmac publishes a supplier onboarding guide and its contract negotiation processes. — [Pharmac: Intro for suppliers](https://www.pharmac.govt.nz/medicine-funding-and-supply/medicine-supply-management/working-with-pharmac-for-medicine-and-medical-device-suppliers/introduction-to-pharmac); [Contract negotiation](https://www.pharmac.govt.nz/medicine-funding-and-supply/the-funding-process/procurement-processes/medicines-and-medical-devices-contract-negotiation)
- Precedent for donor- or sponsor-funded patient connectivity: an ISP founder gifted hospital Wi-Fi in Taranaki, and InspireNet sponsored unlimited Wi-Fi at Whanganui. — [Stuff](https://i.stuff.co.nz/national/health/99723810/isp-founder-gifts-hospital-internet-access-following-diagnosis); [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/whanganui-chronicle/news/whanganui-hospital-to-offer-free-and-unlimited-wi-fi/P3HUIGICAVBARQQHE3OKVNSTKM/)

### Inferences
- Under NZ Government Procurement Rules, open GETS tendering normally applies above about NZ$100k for goods; that threshold is from background knowledge, not verified here. A pilot of 50-200 radios (NZ$5k-25k) would fall well below it, so a ward, service manager or hospital charitable trust could buy directly.
- If the device is not a medical device, Pharmac is not the gatekeeper. Health NZ IT security and network approval (Wi-Fi access, privacy and data sovereignty for a cloud service) is the more likely blocker, and probably the main factor in how long a hospital sale takes.

### Gaps
- No source found on: GETS tenders for patient communication or tablets; All-of-Government panels relevant to devices; typical Health NZ procurement timelines; COVID-era iPad or tablet donations for virtual visits in NZ hospitals or aged care (this is widely believed to have happened, but it is unsourced here).

## 7. Medical alarm market (adjacent benchmark)

### Takeaway
There are three MSD-accredited medical alarm suppliers as of late 2026: VitalCall (Chubb), Freedom and St John, with St John having bought Securely. Monthly costs are about NZ$16-19 a week for in-home alarms and NZ$26-29 a week for "anywhere" mobile alarms. That makes the radio's NZ$2-5 a month subscription very cheap by comparison.
