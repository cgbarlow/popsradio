# Connectivity and digital inclusion among older New Zealanders (gating factor for a Wi-Fi-only device)

Research date: 15 November 2026. Method note: direct page fetches to NZ hosts (comcom.govt.nz, skinny.co.nz, figure.nz) were blocked in this research session (DNS not allowed), so most figures below come from search-engine extracts of primary pages, not full-document reads. Figures marked **[verify]** came from search snippets that could not be checked against the full source. The Commerce Commission 2025 Telecommunications Monitoring Report PDF and the Stats NZ Aotearoa Data Explorer (Census 2023 "access to telecommunication systems" by age and region) should be pulled directly before final numbers are published.

## 1. Share of NZ households with internet at home, by region and by age

### Takeaway
About 90.5% of NZ households had home internet access at the 2023 Census (up from 86.1% in 2018), with most regions at 90–93% and the provincial/rural regions (West Coast, Northland, Gisborne) around 83–86%. I found no 2023 Census figure broken down by age of occupants. The latest age-specific household figure (2013 Census) showed only 49% of people aged 75+ lived in a household with internet access, against 75% of people aged 65–74.

### Cited Findings
**National (Census 2023, households where information was stated):**
- Household internet access was 90.5% (1,473,933 households) in 2023, up from 86.1% (1,313,376) in 2018. Most households (93.5%, 1,522,950) had access to a cellphone. — [Stats NZ, 2023 Census population, dwelling and housing highlights](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/2023-census-population-dwelling-and-housing-highlights/)
- Long-run trend: household internet access was 77% (2013), 86% (2018) and 90% (2023). — [Infometrics, First detailed insights from the 2023 Census (Oct 2024)](https://economics.infometrics.co.nz/article/2024-10-first-detailed-insights-from-the-2023-census)
- By district, access ranged from 96% in Queenstown-Lakes down to 80% in Buller. Most cities and urban areas were above 90%, with lower rates in provincial and rural areas. — [Infometrics (Oct 2024)](https://economics.infometrics.co.nz/article/2024-10-first-detailed-insights-from-the-2023-census)
- By tenure: about 92.6% of owner-occupied households had internet access, against about 85.6% of households not owned and not held in a family trust (mostly renters). — Stats NZ data via search extract [verify] ([Stats NZ highlights](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/2023-census-population-dwelling-and-housing-highlights/))
- Data quality: the response rate for the access-to-telecommunications question was 91.6% on 2023 Census forms. Stats NZ rated the variable "moderate" quality, and noted low response rates for some areas and groups. — [Stats NZ highlights](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/2023-census-population-dwelling-and-housing-highlights/); [Figure.NZ Northland chart](https://figure.nz/chart/wEydlkRVNJcMqwol)

**By age (older data; no 2023 Census age breakdown found):**
- 2013 Census: 84.8% of people under 65 lived in households with internet access, against 75.2% of people aged 65–74 and 49.0% of people aged 75+. — [MSD, The Social Report 2016: Telephone and internet access in the household](https://socialreport.msd.govt.nz/social-connectedness/telephone-and-internet-access-in-the-household.html)
- Digital Council/DIA economic-case research: internet access for people over 75 was 60%. 31% of people living alone did not have home internet. — [Digital Council, Addressing the digital divide: the economic case for increasing digital inclusion](https://www.digital.govt.nz/assets/Digital-government/Digital-inclusion/Digital-Inclusion-Research/Publication-of-Digital-Council-Research-Report-Addressing-the-digital-divide-The-economic-case-for-increasing-digital-inclusion-PDF.pdf)
- Motu/DIA (2017 General Social Survey-based data): more than 35% of people over 75 had no internet access, against almost universal access in the youngest cohort. The access gap is much larger for over-75s than for 65–74-year-olds. — [DIA/Motu, Digital inclusion and wellbeing in New Zealand (Grimes & White, 2019)](https://www.digital.govt.nz/dmsdocument/161~digital-inclusion-and-wellbeing-in-new-zealand/html); [Motu working paper 19-17](https://motu-www.motu.org.nz/wpapers/19_17.pdf)

### Inferences
- The national household rate (90.5%) overstates connectivity for the target users. Older single-person households, and the 75+ group especially, are the least-connected group. Even with strong improvement since 2013 (49% for 75+), a reasonable planning estimate (an **estimate**, not a measured figure) is that roughly 20–30% of people aged 75+ and roughly 10–15% of people aged 65–74 still lived without home internet in 2023. The 85+ group is likely worse.
- Regionally, the addressable "already connected" share is highest in Wellington, Auckland, Canterbury and Queenstown-Lakes, and lowest in West Coast/Buller, Northland and Gisborne. These are also the regions with older and lower-income populations.
- Home internet does not always mean Wi-Fi. Some "internet access" households rely only on a smartphone's mobile data, which would not serve the radio. The Census question does not separate fixed broadband with a Wi-Fi router from mobile-only access.

### Gaps
- 2023 Census internet access by age of occupants (e.g. households where all occupants are 65+) is likely available in Aotearoa Data Explorer (table "Access to telecommunication systems" crossed with household composition), but I could not retrieve it.
- The full 16-region table for 2023 could not be extracted (figure.nz was blocked).
- No 2023/2024 NZ General Social Survey or Household Use of ICT figures were found. Stats NZ's Household Use of ICT survey has not been run since 2012 as far as I could find.

## 2. Share of people 65+, 75+ and 85+ who are offline, and why

### Takeaway
The best available estimates (2017–2021 surveys) put non-users at about 16% of people aged 65+, about 25% of people aged 75–84 and about 50% of people aged 85+. The main reasons are "don't see the purpose" (lack of interest), then lack of skills or knowledge, no device, and cost. There are also strong trust and safety concerns. Nearly half of non-users say they would go online with better access, devices or knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- World Internet Project NZ 2021: 16% of respondents over 65 were internet non-users. 64% of all non-users were over 65. Their most common reason was that they "didn't see the purpose", followed by lack of knowledge, lack of device and cost. 49% of non-users said they would become users with better access to devices, connections or knowledge. — [World Internet Project NZ (AUT/InternetNZ)](https://internetnz.nz/news-and-articles/world-internet-project-new-zealand/); [WIP: Internet use and non-use in NZ](https://nzpri.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/613487/WIP-Users.pdf) [verify exact year mapping]
- 25% of people aged 75–84 did not use the internet, rising to 50% of those aged 85+ (citing Lips et al., 2020 and 2017 research). — [Evaluation of the NZ SilverTech smartphone course for older adults (PMC, 2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12227816/)
- People aged 75+ are nearly five times more likely not to use the internet than younger people. About 56% of people aged 75+ had not used the internet recently (2017-era data). — [DIA/Motu, Digital inclusion and wellbeing](https://www.digital.govt.nz/dmsdocument/161~digital-inclusion-and-wellbeing-in-new-zealand/html)
- DIA's Digital Inclusion Action Plan 2020–21 lists older people, "especially those over 75 years", among the priority groups for digital inclusion. — [DIA, Digital Inclusion Action Plan 2020–2021](https://www.digital.govt.nz/dmsdocument/174~digital-inclusion-action-plan-20202021/html)
- DIA user-insights reports (Māori; Pacific peoples): older people often need help getting online, and younger family members usually provide it. Older Pacific people said they did not know enough to trust that they could use the internet "safely". Kaumātua were described as the age group most susceptible to online scams. — [DIA, Digital inclusion user insights — Māori](https://www.digital.govt.nz/dmsdocument/177~report-digital-inclusion-user-insights-maori/html); [DIA, user insights — Pacific peoples](https://www.digital.govt.nz/dmsdocument/179%E2%88%BCreport-digital-inclusion-user-insights-pacific-peoples/html)
- Research on senior citizens describes a spectrum of engagement, from "highly skilled and engaged" to those who "see no reason to become digitally involved". — [Katoa Connect, Understanding, explaining and self-evaluating digital inclusion and exclusion among senior citizens](https://www.katoaconnect.org.nz/s/Understanding-explaining-and-self-evaluating-digital-inclusion-and-exclusion-among-senior-citizens.pdf)
- WIP NZ 2025: substantial use of digital technologies is concentrated among under-45s. Older New Zealanders express stronger privacy and surveillance concerns and are more likely to be "extremely uncomfortable" with some technologies. — [World Internet Project in NZ 2025 (AUT/InternetNZ)](https://nzpri.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/1073906/WIP-NZ-Report-2025_InternetNZ.pdf)
- InternetNZ Aotearoa Internet Insights 2025 (Verian survey of 1,003 online adults, fieldwork 25 Nov–8 Dec 2025): 76% of people aged 70+ were extremely or very concerned about the security of personal data. The survey covers only people who are online, so it does not measure non-use. — [InternetNZ, Aotearoa Internet Insights 2025](https://internetnz.nz/new-zealands-internet-insights/new-zealands-internet-insights-2025/)
- Citizens Advice Bureau has published a "Face to face with digital exclusion" snapshot of clients excluded from digital-only services. — [CAB infographic](https://www.cab.org.nz/assets/Documents/About-us/Social-Policy/Digital-Exclusion/0ecf2bb64d/Infographic-only_Face-to-Face-with-Digital-Exclusion-A3.pdf) (contents not extracted)

### Inferences
- Lack of interest is the main barrier. That favours the radio's pitch: it needs no skills or interest from the older user. The gate is whether someone else (family) provides a connection. Cost is secondary for the user but real for low-income pensioners.
- Because many offline elders are "not interested", family members are likely the buyer and installer. The connection problem is therefore really the family's problem: "is there Wi-Fi at Nana's?"
- Strong privacy and scam concerns among the 70+ group argue for messaging around a closed, family-only system.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 figure for offline share by 65+/75+/85+ was found. Either the WIP NZ 2025 PDF or a Census 2023 age crosstab is needed.
- The DIA "Digital inclusion user insights — Older people" report (if it exists, published around 2020–21) was not found in search results.
- No Age Concern or 20/20 Trust quantitative figure on older people offline was found in this session.

## 3. Fixed broadband technology mix and coverage by region; Starlink; copper switch-off

### Takeaway
Fibre now reaches about 87% of households, and about 75% of those connected. Outside the fibre footprint, rural broadband is moving fast to Starlink, which had about 85,000 NZ subscribers and 27% of the rural broadband market by June 2025, making it the largest rural ISP. Most rural satellite switchers came straight off copper. Copper is nearly gone: about 12,000 Chorus copper lines were in service at June 2025 (figure needs checking), and Chorus now targets nationwide copper retirement by 2028.

### Cited Findings
- About 87% of households have access to a fibre network, and about 75% of those have taken a fibre connection (estimate in a Feb 2025 report commissioned by the Commerce Commission). — [Feasey, The regulation of fibre-based telecommunications services and networks (20 Feb 2025)](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0026/366326/Feasey-NZ-fibre-regime-final-report-20-February-2025.pdf)
- Starlink's NZ subscribers rose from 58,000 to 85,000 in the year to 30 June 2025, and its share of the rural broadband market rose from 19% to 27%, making it the market leader. 54% of rural consumers who moved to satellite came straight off legacy copper. — [NZ Herald, Starlink becomes NZ's largest rural internet provider (reporting ComCom 2025 Telecommunications Monitoring Report)](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/starlink-becomes-nzs-largest-rural-internet-provider-as-some-local-players-suffer-tech-insider/premium/UFCCHFU64FEMLGTKRJI6Q4NMAQ/); [Telecoms.com, Starlink is New Zealand's biggest rural ISP](https://www.telecoms.com/satellite/starlink-is-new-zealand-s-biggest-rural-isp); [Telecompaper](https://www.telecompaper.com/news/starlink-growth-signals-new-phase-for-new-zealand-telecoms-market-regulator--1575491)
- Starlink earned about NZ$100m from New Zealand in 2025. — [NZ Herald, How Elon Musk's Starlink earned $100m from New Zealand in 2025](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/companies/telecommunications/starlink-revenue-surges-as-sparks-market-share-slips-in-commerce-commission-report-tech-insider/premium/USJQEPRQWBFEPAWCEXPP7A3RYI/)
- The 2025 Telecommunications Monitoring Report (published around 29 June/early July 2026, covering the year to June 2025) finds NZ has "world-class fibre and mobile infrastructure", but customer satisfaction is falling. — [Commerce Commission, 2025 Telecommunications Monitoring Report (PDF)](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/2025-Telecommunications-Monitoring-Report-29-June-2026.pdf); [Scoop, Consumers unsatisfied despite world-class networks (Jul 2026)](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/SC2607/S00011/consumers-unsatisfied-despite-world-class-networks.htm); [Bill Bennett](https://billbennett.co.nz/nz-telecom-customer-satisfaction-declines-comcom-report/)
- The same report says Chorus had about 12,000 copper lines still in service at 30 June 2025. It also reported a rise "from 10% … in June 2024 to 14% in June 2025" in some measure tied to wholesale fibre connections; the metric is unclear in the extract. — [ComCom 2025 TMR via search extract] [verify] ([PDF](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/2025-Telecommunications-Monitoring-Report-29-June-2026.pdf)). Note: 12,000 looks low and may refer only to copper lines inside fibre areas. Check against [Chorus Q3 FY26 connections update (NZX)](https://www.nzx.com/announcements/470904).
- The Commission found nearly 98% of premises are covered by at least three alternative broadband technologies (in its copper-deregulation review). — [Chorus/NZX Q3 FY26 connections update](https://www.nzx.com/announcements/470904) [verify attribution]
- The Commission publishes a Telecommunications Connectivity Map showing coverage by technology and address. — [ComCom Telecommunications Connectivity Map](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/regulated-industries/telecommunications/monitoring-the-telecommunications-market/telecommunications-connectivity-map/)
- Measuring Broadband NZ (independent speed testing for ComCom) publishes performance by technology, including fibre, fixed wireless and satellite. — [Measuring Broadband NZ report, 24 June 2025](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0037/366886/Measuring-Broadband-New-Zealand-Report-24-June-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rural older households switching off copper are choosing among fibre (where available), fixed wireless (4G/5G), and Starlink. All of these deliver Ethernet/Wi-Fi in the home, so a copper switch-off can create new Wi-Fi homes. That makes it a trigger moment for selling the radio ("your landline is going; here's how Nana keeps in touch").
- Starlink routers provide dual-band Wi-Fi including 2.4 GHz (general product knowledge, not sourced here; verify). Satellite latency should be fine for push-to-talk.
- Regional fibre/fixed-wireless/satellite splits were not obtained. The Connectivity Map and the TMR's regional tables are the place to get them.

### Gaps
- Regional broadband technology mix (fibre vs fixed wireless vs satellite vs copper, by region) was not obtained.
- Exact national fixed-broadband connection totals and fibre connection counts for June 2025 and June 2026 were not confirmed.
- Rural Broadband Initiative phase 2 and Crown Infrastructure Partners (now inside MBIE/National Infrastructure Funding & Financing) final coverage figures were not retrieved.

## 4. Cost of the cheapest suitable home connection (2026)

### Takeaway
The cheapest suitable options for an older person with no broadband are, in order:
- **Skinny Jump:** about $5 per 30-day block of 35GB, modem free. Pre-pay 4G wireless broadband for low-income households, with "seniors" explicitly eligible. Accessed through community partners such as libraries. Note an availability pause reported in 2026.
- **Capped 4G wireless plans:** about $45–50/month (Skinny 60GB $45, One NZ 300GB $50, 2degrees 100GB $50, Spark 50GB $50).
- **Unlimited 4G:** about $55/month (Skinny), or One NZ unlimited at $65/month including modem.
- **Cheapest fibre:** Skinny $88/month (or $78 with a Skinny mobile plan); 2degrees and One NZ about $101.

The radio's traffic (about 64 kbit/s, only while talking) fits easily in even 30–35GB a month.

### Cited Findings
**Skinny Jump (concession plan):**
- Eligibility: households with no current home broadband for whom cost is a barrier. Listed groups include job seekers, families with children, people with disabilities, and **seniors**. The address must be in Skinny's 4G coverage area. — [Skinny, About Skinny Jump](https://www.skinny.co.nz/jump/about); [Christchurch City Libraries](https://my.christchurchcitylibraries.com/skinny-jump/); [Selwyn Libraries](https://www.selwynlibraries.co.nz/your-libraries/skinny-jump-helping-you-to-get-online-at-home)
- Price: $5 for 35GB valid 30 days, renewable up to 6 times a month (Christchurch libraries: up to 210GB for $30). An older figure from Whangārei was $5 for 30GB, with modem and first 30GB free. — [Skinny Jump](https://www.skinny.co.nz/jump/about); [Christchurch City Libraries](https://my.christchurchcitylibraries.com/skinny-jump/); [Whangārei District Libraries](https://www.wdc.govt.nz/library/Visit/Library-services/Computers-and-internet/Skinny-Jump)
- Availability: Whangārei District Libraries said the service was "temporarily paused" and expected it back from June 2026. Current status is unconfirmed. — [Whangārei District Libraries](https://www.wdc.govt.nz/library/Visit/Library-services/Computers-and-internet/Skinny-Jump)

### Inferences
- Bandwidth: at 64 kbit/s while transmitting or receiving, one hour of talk is about 29 MB. Even 30 minutes a day is under 1 GB a month (my calculation). So the smallest capped plan, or Skinny Jump's $5/35GB block, is more than enough. Cost is set by plan minimums, not usage.
- Cheapest "add a connection" scenarios for a family (my estimates from the prices above):
  - **(a) Eligible low-income senior:** Skinny Jump, about $5/month, free modem. This depends on community-partner sign-up and on the 2026 pause having ended.
  - **(b) Contract 4G wireless:** about $45/month, or about $540/year, with a free modem.
  - **(c) No-contract:** prepaid mobile SIM in a cheap 4G Wi-Fi router. Hardware about NZ$60–150 retail (unsourced, verify), plus a prepay data pack. I did not find a dedicated prepay home-broadband product from One NZ or Spark.
- Key check for every router option: 2.4 GHz WPA2-Personal must be enabled. Most 4G/5G modems (e.g. One NZ SmartWiFi, Skinny's modems) are dual-band; verify per model.
- Both Skinny Jump and wireless broadband require 4G coverage at the address. Rural no-coverage homes would need Starlink: the Residential Lite and Standard plans are around NZ$70–120/month in 2026 (unsourced, verify), plus hardware.

### Gaps
- Starlink NZ 2026 plan prices and hardware cost were not sourced.
- Prepay data-only SIM prices (One NZ, Spark, 2degrees, Skinny prepay data packs) and IoT/M2M data plans were not sourced.
- Retail price of entry-level 4G Wi-Fi routers (e.g. at Noel Leeming or PB Tech) was not sourced.
- Skinny Jump user numbers and current (Nov 2026) availability were not confirmed. skinny.co.nz could not be fetched.

## 5. Landline use among older people and the end of copper landlines

### Takeaway
Landlines are now a minority technology: only 31–42% of households in Northland, Gisborne and West Coast had one in 2023. Older people are the heaviest remaining landline users, and copper is ending (fibre areas by end of 2026, nationwide by 2028). Replacement voice services (fibre/wireless VoIP) don't work in power cuts without a battery backup. This gives the radio an opening as a simple family-contact device, but it must not be pitched as a 111 or emergency replacement.

### Cited Findings
- Landline access, 2023 Census (% of households): Gisborne 31.49%, Northland 35.08%, West Coast 42.12%. — [Figure.NZ, Households with access to a landline phone in NZ](https://figure.nz/chart/aqr9KjNxvA6jeX1u)
- Copper retirement timeline and Copper Withdrawal Code protections are in Section 3. — [TCF](https://www.tcf.org.nz/digital-living/copper-retirement); [Scoop Business (Apr 2026)](https://business.scoop.co.nz/2026/04/19/chorus-accelerates-copper-network-shutdown/)
- Consumer guidance aimed at seniors explains what happens at copper switch-off and what to do. — [Senior Gadget Guide NZ, Copper landline switch-off](https://seniorgadgetguide.co.nz/copper-landline-retirement-nz/)
- The 111 Contact Code protects vulnerable consumers (free means of calling 111 in a power cut). — [Orbital Nodes summary](https://orbitalnodes.ai/coverage/nz-copper-retirement)

### Inferences
- Older people losing copper must move to a fibre or wireless home phone, and either one installs a home router with Wi-Fi. A copper switch-off therefore leaves Wi-Fi in homes that previously had only a landline.
- The national landline share for 2023 was not extracted, but given the regional figures it is likely well under half of households (estimate).

### Gaps
- No NZ landline-use-by-age statistic for 2023 (e.g. share of 75+ households with landline only) was found.
- No count of older consumers registered as "vulnerable" under the 111 Contact Code was found.

## 6. Wi-Fi in rest homes, retirement villages and hospitals

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly anecdotal. Some large operators (e.g. Radius Care) now advertise free personal Wi-Fi for residents, but InternetNZ-cited research found aged care facilities "seldom provided" resident Wi-Fi. I found no NZ data on how common captive portals are in rest homes or hospitals.

### Cited Findings
- Radius Waipuna (Auckland) and Radius Millstream (Ashburton) list free personal Wi-Fi for residents "to stay connected with loved ones". — [Eldernet: Radius Waipuna](https://www.eldernet.co.nz/provider/radius-waipuna/residential-care); [Eldernet: Radius Millstream](https://www.eldernet.co.nz/provider/radius-millstream/residential-care)
- Glendale Retirement Home (Dunedin) lists Wi-Fi with Skype, plus a residents' iPad. — [Eldernet: Glendale](https://www.eldernet.co.nz/provider/glendale-retirement-home/residential-care)
- InternetNZ-funded research: a digital divide persists for older people in residential aged care, and facilities seldom provided Wi-Fi access for residents. Family often fills the gap. The article is several years old. — [InternetNZ, Digital divide still exists for older people living in residential aged care](https://internetnz.nz/news-and-articles/digital-divide-still-exists-in-new-zealand-for-older-people-living-in-residential-aged-care/)
- IT providers market managed networks to NZ rest homes and villages. — [Exodesk, IT for aged care](https://exodesk.co.nz/it-for-aged-care/)
- What's included in rest home fees (including any Wi-Fi) is set out in the admission agreement. — [Eldernet, Are rest homes free?](https://www.eldernet.co.nz/knowledge-lab/financial-legal-issues/whats-the-cost/are-rest-homes-free-for-the-elderly); [Health NZ, Residential care](https://www.healthnz.govt.nz/hospitals-services/services-support/older-people/residential-care)

### Inferences
- Facility Wi-Fi is often a guest network with a click-through or sign-in portal, which the radio can't use (an assumption based on general practice; no NZ data). For residents in care, a family-supplied 4G router in the room, or a pre-configured WPA2 resident SSID from the operator, may be needed. Operator partnerships (Radius, Ryman, Summerset, Oceania, Arvida, Bupa) are worth exploring.
- Public hospital (Health NZ) patient Wi-Fi is typically a captive-portal guest network (assumption, not sourced). The radio should be treated as unsupported in hospitals.

### Gaps
- No survey of resident Wi-Fi prevalence or captive portals across NZ aged-residential-care or retirement villages was found.
- No Health NZ patient Wi-Fi policy document was found.

## 7. Mobile coverage and rural blackspots (context)

### Takeaway
The Rural Connectivity Group (a Spark, One NZ and 2degrees joint venture, Crown co-funded) has built about 450–500 rural cell sites. By April 2025 the programmes had added coverage to about 2,011 km of state highway, 2,897 km of local roads and 192 tourism blackspots. Gaps remain, especially in Otago and Southland, and a May 2026 outage took down about 90 RCG sites. This matters because 4G wireless broadband and Skinny Jump both depend on 4G coverage at the home.

### Cited Findings
- As of 30 April 2025, the RCG/Mobile Black Spot programmes had brought coverage to 2,011 km of state highways, 2,897 km of local roads and 192 tourism blackspots, track-ends and cycleways. — [Reseller News, Finish line in sight for $770M rural connectivity programme](https://www.reseller.co.nz/article/4038514/finish-line-in-sight-for-770m-rural-connectivity-programme.html)
- RCG has passed 450 live sites (later update: 480). Wikipedia cites 500 sites by July 2024. — [Reseller News](https://www.reseller.co.nz/article/4038514/finish-line-in-sight-for-770m-rural-connectivity-programme.html); [Wikipedia, Rural Connectivity Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_Connectivity_Group)
- Otago highway dead-zone elimination reached 149 km, 102% of target. — [Crux, Lindis, The Neck, Makarora](https://crux.org.nz/crux-news/lindis-the-neck-makarora-highway-mobile-black-spots-eliminated)
- 2026: Southern tower targets are met, but some Otago/Southland farmers still can't call 111. — [B2B News, Otago, Southland mobile blackspots persist despite towers](https://b2bnews.co.nz/news/otago-southland-mobile-blackspots-persist-despite-towers/)
- May 2026: about 90 RCG sites failed, causing hours-long mobile and internet outages across all carriers in parts of the South Island and lower North Island. — [Newswire (May 2026)](https://newswire.co.nz/2026/05/south-island-and-lower-north-hit-by-hours-long-mobile-and-internet-outage-as-rural-connectivity-group-towers-fail/)
- CIP briefing to the incoming Minister (May 2024) covers the RBI2, Mobile Black Spot Fund and RCG programmes. — [CIP BIM, May 2024](https://www.nationalinfrastructure.govt.nz/wp-content/uploads/CIP-BIM-Media-and-Comms-May-2024-Redacted.pdf) (contents not extracted)
- The RCG's goal was about a 25% increase in mobile land-area coverage, not population coverage. — [Wairoa DC, Rural Connectivity Group](https://www.wairoadc.govt.nz/our-council/news/archive/article/232/rural-connectivity-group)

### Inferences
- Population 4G coverage in NZ is commonly quoted at about 98–99% (general knowledge, not sourced here). The remaining rural homes without 4G at the house are where Starlink is the only practical way to add Wi-Fi, at much higher cost than about $45/month wireless.

### Gaps
- No population-coverage % by region for 4G/5G was found.
- No list of blackspot regions was found beyond Otago/Southland anecdotes.
