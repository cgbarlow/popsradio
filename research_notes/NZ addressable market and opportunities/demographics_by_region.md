## 1. Population aged 65+, 75+, 85+: national and by region (Census 2023, latest estimates, projections)

### Takeaway
NZ had about **829,000 people aged 65+ at the 2023 Census (16.6%)**. By mid-2025 the figure was **about 900,000 (1 in 6)**, and it is projected to pass **1 million by 2029**, reach about **1 in 5 by the mid-2030s** and reach **1.5 million in the early 2050s**. Auckland holds about 26.5% of the 65+ population (219,753). The oldest areas by share are Thames-Coromandel (34% aged 65+), Kāpiti and Kaikōura (27%), and the West Coast districts. I could not retrieve verified 65+ counts for the other 15 regions. A clearly flagged modelled table is given under Inferences.

## 2. Living alone; private dwellings vs residential care

### Takeaway
At the 2023 Census, **192,201 people aged 65+ lived alone (25.3% of over-65s in households)**, two-thirds of them women. About **32,000 older people are in subsidised aged residential care (ARC)**, so roughly **96% of over-65s live in private dwellings**. The ARC rate climbs steeply with age: 26.4% of people aged 90+ are in care.

### Cited Findings
- 2023 Census: **389,352 people lived alone** (8.5% of people in households). **49.4% of them (192,201) were aged 65+**, meaning about **25.3% of people aged 65+ lived alone**, down from 28.8% in 2013. **127,536 (66.4%) were women** and 64,413 were men. One-person households were 22.8% of households in occupied private dwellings — [Stats NZ, 2023 Census household, family & extended family highlights](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/2023-census-household-family-and-extended-family-highlights/); [Stats NZ "How we live"](https://www.stats.govt.nz/news/how-we-live-a-look-at-families-and-households-from-the-2023-census/)
- About 400,000 Kiwis live alone (business framing) — [B2B News](https://b2bnews.co.nz/news/400000-kiwis-live-alone-what-it-means-for-business/)
- ARC: in 2022–23 the government paid **$1.4b subsidising ARC for 32,000 New Zealanders**. Residents by care level: rest home about 42%, hospital about 42%, dementia about 15%, psychogeriatric about 3%. **26.4% of people aged 90+ were in residential care**, and **over 70% of ARC residents are aged 80+** — [Ministry of Health, Aged Care Ministerial Advisory Group report – key facts & figures (Aug 2026)](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2026-08/aged-care-ministerial-advisory-group-report-key-facts-figures.pdf); [NZ Listener/Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/the-listener/new-zealand/aged-care-crisis-too-few-beds-rising-costs-and-a-failing-health-system/MVXSJQG7VZE57MROWKWAO4F7RY)
- NZ faces a projected shortfall of **12,000 ARC beds by 2032** — [NZ Herald/Listener](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/the-listener/new-zealand/aged-care-crisis-too-few-beds-rising-costs-and-a-failing-health-system/MVXSJQG7VZE57MROWKWAO4F7RY)
- Retirement village residents are overwhelmingly Pākehā/European (no count in the extract) — [The Spinoff, Aug 2026](https://thespinoff.co.nz/society/10-08-2026/nzs-retirement-village-population-is-almost-entirely-white-where-is-everyone-else)

### Inferences
- Private-dwelling over-65s ≈ 829k − ~32–35k in ARC ≈ **~795k (2023)**, or **~865k (2026)**.
- Living-alone over-65s in 2026 ≈ 192k × 1.10 ≈ **~210k**. These are the highest-value users, because loneliness and a "check-in" need are strongest here.
- Regional living-alone split (estimate): if the 25% rate applies roughly evenly, apply 25% to the regional 65+ counts in section 1, e.g. Auckland about 55k, Canterbury about 28k, Waikato about 21k. Auckland's rate is probably lower because of its multi-generational Pacific and Asian households.
- ARC residents are a secondary channel (facility-installed units). Only rest-home-level residents (about 42% of about 32k, i.e. about 13k) are likely to be able to use the device.

### Gaps
- Regional living-alone counts for 65+ (available in Census 2023 tables on ADE) were not retrieved.
- Total retirement-village resident numbers (the RVA usually cites about 50,000 residents) were not retrieved in this session.

## 3. Conditions that make smartphones hard (hearing, vision, dementia, dexterity, digital literacy)

### Takeaway
The barriers are large. Self-reported hearing impairment affects **about 24% of 65–74s and about 39% of 75+**. Dementia affected **about 70,000 people in 2020 and about 83,000 in 2025**, projected to reach **about 167,000 (10.8% of 65+) by 2050**. Roughly **a third of over-75s had no internet access (2018/19 data)**, and **about 50% of over-85s don't use the internet**. **79% of over-80s lack "essential" digital skills.** A hearing-heavy profile matters for a voice-only device: it needs to be loud and clear, and adjustable volume is critical.

### Cited Findings
**Dementia**
- **About 70,000 people with dementia in 2020** (up 7,000 since 2016), projected to reach **167,483 by 2050**, which is **2.7% of the total population and 10.8% of people aged 65+**. Cost was about $2.5b in 2020, rising to about $5.9–6b/yr by 2050. Unpaid care was about 53 million hours/yr, valued at $1.19b — [Dementia Economic Impact Report 2020 (Univ. of Auckland for Alzheimers NZ, pub. Sep 2021)](https://cdn.alzheimers.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Dementia-Economic-Impact-Report-2020.pdf); [Alzheimers NZ](https://alzheimers.org.nz/news/spike-in-dementia-cases-to-generate-major-health-equity-issues/); [Newstalk ZB](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/national/dementia-cases-to-surge-to-170-000-new-report)
- A newer estimate puts **about 83,000 NZers living with dementia** in 2025, about 40,000 of whom need care. It is based on international prevalence rates, so the true figure is likely higher — [RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/chinese/573525/families-missing-early-dementia-signs-as-projections-soar); [NZCCSS Snapshot: Aged Care (May 2026)](https://nzccss.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Snapshot-Aged-Care.pdf)
- Māori dementia cases are projected to rise faster than other groups' (a health-equity concern) — [Te Ao Māori News](https://www.teaomaori.news/study-predicts-spike-maori-dementia)

**Hearing**
- National study (n=16,080, 2018 publication): **23.5% of 65–74s and 39.4% of those aged 75+ reported hearing impairment**. Disability Survey 2013: **32% of Māori aged 65+ vs 28% of all NZ 65+ reported hearing loss** — [NZMJ scoping review, Hearing loss among older Māori](https://nzmj.org.nz/journal/vol-134-no-1535/hearing-loss-and-hearing-service-experiences-among-older-maori-and-whanau-a-scoping-review)
- Frail community-dwelling older adults (interRAI home-care assessments 2019–20, mean age 82): **19% had hearing impairment** and **10% had vision impairment** (moderate or worse); **7% had dual sensory impairment** — [PubMed 41231480, Epidemiology of Vision and Hearing Impairment in Older Community-Dwelling Adults in NZ](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41231480/)
- All-ages estimate (2020): about 880,000 NZers have hearing loss and about 180,000 have moderate-to-severe functional vision loss (no age split in the extract) — [mivision, Feb 2026](https://digital.mivision.com.au/collections/mivision-magazine-february-2026/concerning-gaps-in-elderly-vision-and-hearing-5q01)
- Hearing and vision testing drops off once people enter residential care — [Medical Xpress, Dec 2025](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-12-vision-residential.html)

**Vision**
- Survey of NZers aged 75+: **30% of men and 30% of women reported vision disability** (self-reported, older study) — [Zhang et al., Australasian J. on Ageing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24521436/)
- Blind Low Vision NZ (Disability Survey and IDI, 2023): people with severe or other visual impairment are twice as likely as others to be aged 65+. No direct 65+ prevalence was given in the extract — [Blind Low Vision NZ](https://blindlowvision.org.nz/shining-a-light-on-blindness/)

**Digital literacy and internet use**
- Motu (Grimes & White, 2019, using GSS data): **more than 35% of over-75s have no internet access**, against more than 99% access for the youngest cohort. MSD later cited this as **25% of over-65s and 35% of over-75s with no internet connection** — [Motu working paper 19-17](https://motu-www.motu.org.nz/wpapers/19_17.pdf); [MSD OIA response, 2022](https://www.msd.govt.nz/documents/about-msd-and-our-work/publications-resources/official-information-responses/2022/march/11-3-2022-information-on-processes-used-by-the-office-over-the-last-three-years-to-consult-with-those-living-in-retirement-lifestyle-villages-r-rep217800-.pdf)
- Lips et al. (2020): **25% of 75–84s and 50% of over-85s did not use the internet** — cited in [SilverTech smartphone course evaluation (PMC, 2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12227816/)
- Business NZ/BNA 2021 survey: **33% of 60–69s, about 50% of 70–79s and 79% of over-80s lacked an "essential" level of digital skills** — [MSD OIA response](https://www.msd.govt.nz/documents/about-msd-and-our-work/publications-resources/official-information-responses/2022/march/11-3-2022-information-on-processes-used-by-the-office-over-the-last-three-years-to-consult-with-those-living-in-retirement-lifestyle-villages-r-rep217800-.pdf)
- Attitudes to Ageing survey: 20% of over-75s did not access the internet at all and another 5% used it less than weekly — [MSD OIA response](https://www.msd.govt.nz/documents/about-msd-and-our-work/publications-resources/official-information-responses/2022/march/11-3-2022-information-on-processes-used-by-the-office-over-the-last-three-years-to-consult-with-those-living-in-retirement-lifestyle-villages-r-rep217800-.pdf)
- Digital Government NZ has a digital inclusion and wellbeing research page — [digital.govt.nz](https://www.digital.govt.nz/dmsdocument/161~digital-inclusion-and-wellbeing-in-new-zealand/html)

### Inferences
- The figures applied below are mid-range rates, not measured overlaps. For 75+ in 2026 (about 420k): hearing impaired about 39% ≈ **~165k**. Not using the internet about 25–35% ≈ **~105–145k**. Lacking essential digital skills (70–79: about 50%; 80+: 79%) is a large majority. People with dementia ≈ 83k+ (most of them 75+, roughly half in the community).
- Digital-access data is 2018–2021 and the gap has probably narrowed since COVID. Even so, the *digital skills* gap among over-80s (79%) is the strongest evidence for a one-button product.
- An important no-internet caveat: the product needs home Wi-Fi. The roughly 25–35% of over-75s with no home connection may need a bundled cellular/Wi-Fi hotspot option, or the family supplies broadband.

### Gaps
- No NZ arthritis/hand-dexterity prevalence for 65+ was retrieved (Arthritis NZ commonly cites about 670,000 people with arthritis of all ages; not verified here).
- No smartphone ownership rate for 75+ was found. InternetNZ and 20/20 Trust/DIAA surveys from 2024–26 were not reached.
- No NZ Health Survey 2023/24 or 2024/25 figures by age were retrieved (health.govt.nz was blocked).
- No regional prevalence rates were found. Apply the national rates to the regional 65+ and 75+ counts.

## 4. Loneliness and social isolation

### Takeaway
About **11% of over-65s report feeling lonely at least some of the time** (NZGSS 2023), and loneliness is **slightly higher among over-75s than 65–74s** (2016: 13% of over-75s). Over-65s are less lonely than young adults in percentage terms, but they are the group that lives alone most.

### Cited Findings
- NZ General Social Survey 2023: **over 11% of people aged 65+ felt lonely most, all, or some of the time** — [ODT, "Fostering connection to beat loneliness"](https://www.odt.co.nz/canterbury/fostering-connection-to-beat-loneliness-ik8ebru4)
- Office for Seniors, Better Later Life indicators 2026 update: loneliness is **slightly higher for those aged 75+ than for those aged 65–74** (chart values were not extractable) — [Office for Seniors (PDF)](https://www.osc.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/Better-Later-Life-indicators-2026-update-enhancing-opportunities-for-participation-and-social-connection-PDF-202-KB.pdf)
- Social Report 2016: **13% of over-75s feel lonely all, most, or some of the time** — via [Loneliness NZ](https://www.loneliness.org.nz/nz/facts/loneliness-prevalent-in-the-vulnerable)
- Age Concern has called for a "Ministry for Loneliness" to support older NZers — [Newstalk ZB](https://newstalkzb.co.nz/news/national/the-lonely-statistics-age-concern-calls-for-ministry-for-loneliness-to-support-elderly-new-zealanders); [Age Concern loneliness research](https://www.ageconcern.org.nz/Public/Info/Research/Loneliness_and_Social_Isolation_Research.aspx)
- Absolute numbers of lonely over-75s are small compared with other age groups — [The Spinoff, Jan 2026](https://thespinoff.co.nz/society/03-01-2026/are-new-zealanders-getting-lonelier-2)
- Baseline source: Loneliness in NZ, findings from the 2010 NZGSS — [Social Wellbeing Agency](https://thehub.sia.govt.nz/resources/loneliness-in-new-zealand-findings-from-the-2010-nz-general-social-survey)

### Inferences
- About 11–13% of about 900k over-65s ≈ **~100–120k lonely older NZers (2025/26)**. They overlap heavily with the about 210k who live alone.
- The NZGSS samples private households only, so it excludes ARC residents, and it under-represents people with dementia or hearing loss. True isolation is likely understated.

### Gaps
- No exact 2023 NZGSS figure for 75+, and no regional loneliness rates (the NZGSS has limited regional power).

## 5. Ethnic breakdown and rural/urban split

### Takeaway
The older population is predominantly European/Pākehā. There were **about 65,900 Māori aged 65+ in June 2023** (up from 50,100 in 2018), about 8% of all over-65s. Pacific and Asian over-65s are fast-growing but smaller groups, concentrated in Auckland. The 65+ share of every ethnic group is projected to rise.

### Cited Findings
- Estimated Māori population aged 65+: **65,900 at 30 June 2023**, up from **50,100 in 2018** — [Figure.NZ / Stats NZ, Estimated Māori population](https://figure.nz/chart/w4vKCUNAR47iOzZj-0zF9f9Hj3jKE6LZv)
- Auckland: **11,907 Māori aged 65+** (5.8% of Auckland Māori), up 30.2% since 2018 — [Knowledge Auckland, Māori 2023 Census summary](https://knowledgeauckland.org.nz/media/hq5hw3ip/māori-2023-census-summary-auckland.pdf)
- Projections show every ethnic group will have more people, and a higher share of its population, aged 65+ — [Figure.NZ projections, Māori 65+](https://figure.nz/chart/oHzkZyiVbVThuQ9u); [Pacific 65+](https://figure.nz/chart/SxZRXd0THb6IfPtp); [Asian 65+](https://figure.nz/chart/YQjOaR2ZUcmQs5KI)
- National median age was 38.1 in 2023 (37.4 in 2018) — [Newstalk ZB](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/national/census-2023-results-new-zealand-s-population-growing-diversifying-and-ageing)

### Inferences
- Kaumātua (about 66k in 2023, probably about 75k in 2026) are concentrated in Northland, Waikato, BoP, Gisborne and Hawke's Bay. They are a culturally distinct segment (te reo UI and whānau-group channels), and their hearing-loss rate is higher (32% vs 28%).
- Asian over-65s (many are parents brought in on parent-category visas) have children who may live overseas, which fits the cross-border segment (section 6).
- Rural/urban: the oldest areas are mostly small towns and rural districts (Thames-Coromandel, Kāpiti, Kaikōura, Buller, Westland, Carterton). Check Wi-Fi/broadband coverage there.

### Gaps
- No verified Census 2023 counts of Pacific, Asian or European over-65s; no regional ethnic splits for 65+.
- No rural vs urban (Stats NZ urban-rural classification) split of over-65s by region was retrieved.

## 6. Households with an older person; family "buyer" pool; diaspora

### Takeaway
No source directly counts "households containing a 65+ person" or "adult-child buyer households". Modelled, there are about **500–550k private households with someone aged 65+**. Abroad, about **915,000 NZ-born people live overseas** (two-thirds in Australia) and about **65–74k people a year leave**, which creates a large cross-border family segment.

### Cited Findings
- Treasury (2025) estimates the **NZ-born diaspora at about 915,000, about two-thirds in Australia**. There are "nearly a million diasporic birth citizens", and people with rights to return number many more — [The Star / Treasury Analytical Note AN25/10, Nov 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2025/11/04/nz-diaspora-quantified-as-treasury-assesses-risk); [IDEAS/RePEc, Treasury AN25/10](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nzt/nztans/an25-10.html)
- Record migrant departures: **73,900 in the year to Aug 2025**. Net loss of NZ citizens was a record **47,900 (Aug 2025 year)**. About 60% of departing citizens go to Australia. **39% of citizen departures (27,200 of 70,000) were aged 18–30 (Mar 2025 year)** — [Newstalk ZB](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/business/migration-kiwis-still-leaving-new-zealand-in-record-numbers); [Scoop / Stats NZ International Migration](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/AK2506/S00259.htm); [MacroBusiness](https://www.macrobusiness.com.au/2025/06/brain-drain-sees-young-kiwis-flee-to-australia)

### Inferences (all estimates)
- **Households with a 65+ resident (about 2026):** about 865k over-65s in private dwellings. About 210k live alone, which gives about 210k one-person households. Of the remaining about 655k, most live in couples, often both aged 65+, giving roughly 280–330k further households. The total is **about 500–550k households**.
- **Buyer pool:** NZ's current older cohorts (born 1940s–1960s, part of the baby-boom era) typically had 2–3 children. So each older household plausibly maps to about 2–3 adult-child households, plus grandchildren. That is about 1–1.5m potential "buyer" households in NZ and abroad (very rough).
- **Cross-border:** the about 915k NZ-born overseas are mostly working age, so many have parents in NZ, and recent departures skew young. Migrants in NZ with parents overseas are a further segment (unquantified).

### Gaps
- No official count of households containing someone aged 65+ (Census 2023 household-composition tables on ADE would give it).
- No NZ estimate of adult children per older person, or of parents of migrants living abroad.

## 7. Income and wealth of older NZers (willingness to pay)

### Takeaway
NZ Super is universal at 65. From 1 April 2026 it pays **NZ$555/week net for a single person living alone** and **NZ$854/week net for a couple**. A NZ$100–250 pair costs a solo pensioner about 2–5 days of Super. In most cases, though, the buyer is an adult child.

### Cited Findings
- NZ Super from 1 April 2026 (M tax code): **single, living alone NZ$647.37 gross / NZ$555.15 net per week** (NZ$1,110.30 net per fortnight). **Single, sharing NZ$595.57 / NZ$512.45**. **Couple, each NZ$492.14 / NZ$427.04**, so **NZ$984.28 gross / NZ$854.08 net combined**. Next review 1 April 2027 — [Work and Income, How much you can get for NZ Super](https://www.workandincome.govt.nz/eligibility/seniors/superannuation/how-much-you-can-get); [Work and Income benefit rates April 2026](https://www.workandincome.govt.nz/products/benefit-rates/benefit-rates-april-2026); [Sorted](https://sorted.org.nz/a-z-guides/nz-super-rates)
- In 2023, 23–24% of over-65s in most regions were employed (Gisborne 29.1%, Nelson 19.8%) — [Stats NZ](https://www.stats.govt.nz/news/working-lives-are-getting-longer/)

### Inferences
- A NZ$2–5/month channel fee is about 0.1–0.2% of a single pensioner's net Super, which is affordable. A one-off NZ$35–99 channel is about 1–4 days of Super.

### Gaps
- No home-ownership rate for over-65s (Census 2023), and no HES 2023/24 wealth or income figures by age, were retrieved. Office for Seniors' Better Later Life indicators and Stats NZ Household Economic Survey (blocked) are the sources to check.
