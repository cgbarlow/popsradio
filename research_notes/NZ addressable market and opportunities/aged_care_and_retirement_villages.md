> Research date: 15 November 2026. **Method caveat:** in this session, direct page or PDF fetching was blocked by network policy. That covers health.govt.nz (the certified-provider CSV), the Westpac PDF and the operator PDFs. All findings below come from web-search result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full documents. Figures marked "(verify)" should be checked against the primary document before being used in a pitch. The best single primary source for regional ARC beds is the Health NZ / Ministry of Health certified rest-home provider CSV (https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/LegalEntitySummaryAgedCare.csv), reportedly updated 13 Nov 2026. ## 1. Aged residential care (ARC): facilities, beds, bed types, occupancy, residents (national and regional)

### Takeaway
NZ has about 40,000–41,500 ARC beds in roughly 670+ facilities (2025–26). Occupancy has tightened from ~87% to over 92% nationally, and to 95–98% in hot-spot regions such as Bay of Plenty and Hutt Valley. About 13% of beds are dementia and about 2% psychogeriatric. That implies roughly 37,000–38,000 ARC residents at any time (estimate). I could not obtain a district-level bed table from accessible sources; the Health NZ certified-provider CSV is the way to build one.

### Cited Findings
**National size**
- The Aged Care Association (ACA, formerly NZACA) 2025 submissions cite "more than 40,000 beds across over 670 aged residential care facilities" (2025) — [ACA submission to Health Select Committee, Pae Ora, 2025](https://www.aca.org.nz/Common/Uploaded%20files/Our%20Work/Submissions/2025-18%20Pae%20Ora%20(Healthy%20Futures)%20-final.pdf); [ACA submission on pay equity, 2025](https://www.aca.org.nz/Common/Uploaded%20files/Our%20Work/Submissions/2025-07%20ACA%20-%20Peoples%20SC%20on%20pay%20equity.pdf)

**Bed types**
- 2023 data from the ACA 2024 sector profile: 13.2% of the ~40,000 ARC beds are dedicated to dementia care and 2.3% to psychogeriatric care. ### Inferences
- **Resident count (estimate):** 41,500 beds × ~92% occupancy ≈ 38,000 ARC residents at any point. Turnover is high (ARC stays are often under 2–3 years), so the number of people passing through each year is larger.
- **Facility count:** ~670 facilities is the realistic number of B2B "sites". Average size is ~60 beds per facility (40,000 ÷ 670, derived).
- **Regional distribution (estimate, unverified):** absent the CSV, a reasonable working assumption is that ARC beds follow the 75+/85+ population. That puts Auckland at roughly 25–30%, Canterbury ~13–15%, Waikato ~9–10%, Bay of Plenty ~7–8%, Wellington region ~9–10%, and Otago/Southland ~7%. **These are assumptions, not sourced figures;** replace them with CSV sums.
- About a third to half of beds are held by a handful of corporates, so a few deals could reach a large share of beds. The long tail (~66% of beds by the ACA definition) is fragmented across charitable, faith-based and family operators. Those would need channel sales via the ACA, distributors or nurse-call resellers.
- About 15% of beds are dementia or psychogeriatric. Residents there may struggle even with a one-button device, but the "knob picks who, hold to talk" design is a plausible fit for some mild-to-moderate dementia residents with family contact. This is a hypothesis to test with dementia-unit staff.

### Gaps
- No district-by-district bed table (rest home / hospital / dementia / psychogeriatric) could be retrieved. The MoH CSV and the HNZ00202368 OIA release are the sources to use.
- No current split of rest-home versus hospital-level beds was found.
- I could not access a 2025 or 2026 edition of the ACA/BERL sector profile.

## 2. Retirement villages: villages, units, residents, penetration, regional split

### Takeaway
JLL's latest database (year to Dec 2024, published Dec 2025) counts **491 villages with 43,598 units**, up from 343 villages and 21,815 units in 2012. About 14–15% of New Zealanders aged 75+ live in villages (14.7% in 2024). Penetration is highest in Bay of Plenty (21.0% in 2024) and lowest in Southland (6.6% in 2023). The resident count is likely around 55,000 (estimate). Auckland, Canterbury and Waikato dominate the development pipeline.

### Inferences
- **Resident count (estimate):** 43,598 units × 1.3 persons ≈ 56,700 residents at end-2024, less vacant or unsold stock. Oceania alone reports $227m of unsold stock. So **~50,000–56,000 village residents** is a reasonable 2025–26 range, flagged as an estimate.
- Villages (~491 sites, ~89 units on average, derived) suit the "villa resident checks in with the village centre" use case. Many villa residents are independent, own smartphones (per Summerset; see Q3) and may prefer apps. The radio's best fit is likely older or frailer villa and serviced-apartment residents, and couples where one partner is in the care centre.
- The regions to prioritise for village pilots are Bay of Plenty (highest penetration, Tauranga/Papamoa growth), Auckland (largest stock and pipeline) and Canterbury (many small villages, strong growth).

## 4. Funding, operator purchasing ability, nurse call / alarm systems and procurement

### Takeaway
ARC is funded by Health NZ under a national ARC agreement with four bed-day rates. For 2025/26 the sector received a 4% price uplift (+$69m) plus 1.24% volume growth (~$96m total increase). Prices are regulated and capped for rest-home care, so margins are thin and discretionary spend on resident amenity devices will be scrutinised. Village operators, funded by ORA capital and fees, have more freedom. Nurse call in NZ is supplied by local specialists (Nurse Call NZ, Nurse Call Plus/Advance Care, Miracle Electronics CareCall), usually with service contracts and reseller networks.

### Cited Findings
- Oct 2025: the ARC sector received a 4% funding uplift. The total increase this year is $96m (4% price = $69m, plus 1.24% volume growth = $21.9m). Home and community support services get +$44m. The combined ARC + HCSS budget is $2,545.35m — [Scoop/Beehive, Oct 2025](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PA2510/S00002/aged-care-funding-and-service-improvements.htm)
- Health NZ was working with the sector to update the ARC contract (Oct 2025) — [Scoop, Oct 2025](https://community.scoop.co.nz/2025/10/aged-care-funding-and-service-improvements/)
- Health NZ increased ARC funding by $145.5m a year for 2026-27 (reported with Bay of Plenty occupancy) — [SunLive](https://www.sunlive.co.nz/news/386066-tauranga-faces-aged-care-bed-shortfall.html) (verify)
- Treasury: Health NZ caps ARC prices for rest-home care — [Treasury OIA-20250657, Oct 2025](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2025-10/oia-20250657.pdf)

- Nurse call suppliers in NZ:
  - **Nurse Call New Zealand:** NZ-based, manufactures and sources nurse call products for rest homes, hospitals and aged care, and sets up accounts for NZ rest homes — [Nurse Call NZ](https://www.nursecallnewzealand.co.nz/); [Products](https://www.nursecallnewzealand.co.nz/products/products.html)
  - **Nurse Call Plus:** NZ supplier of the Advance Care system, with tiered service and maintenance agreements (Tier 2 adds proactive maintenance and unscheduled call-out cover) — [Nurse Call Plus](https://nursecallplus.co.nz/); [System servicing](https://nursecallplus.co.nz/products-and-features/system-servicing)
  - **Miracle Electronics:** Kiwi-owned. Its CareCall system serves retirement living and aged care in NZ and Australia through a reseller network, and is designed to meet Ngā Paerewa (NZS 8134:2021) — [Miracle Electronics](https://miracle-electronics.com/)
- Aged-care IT guidance lists nurse call and call bell systems, and anything integrated with them, as systems "that can never be allowed to stop". Care management software covers clinical records, rostering, billing and nurse call — [Exodesk, IT for aged care](https://exodesk.co.nz/it-for-aged-care/)

### Inferences
- **Positioning:** because nurse call is a compliance-critical, Ngā Paerewa-linked system, the radio should be sold as a *complement* (social and non-urgent contact, "can you bring me a cup of tea") rather than a nurse-call replacement. Otherwise it would face certification, reliability and liability hurdles. Integrating with or reselling through nurse-call resellers is a possible channel.
- **Price point:** NZ$50–125 per radio is small against per-bed revenue and against nurse-call installs. Bulk purchase of ~60 units per facility would cost ~NZ$3,000–7,500, likely within a facility manager's or activities budget rather than needing group capex approval (inference). A NZ$2–5/month channel fee per resident could be passed through as an optional resident-paid extra, a common model for TV/phone/Wi-Fi in villages (not verified).
- Under capped ARC funding, family-paid or resident-paid purchase (the family buys the "Pop's Radio" pair, with the facility approving Wi-Fi access) may be an easier route in ARC than operator-funded bulk purchase.

## 5. Resident Wi-Fi, captive portals, and loneliness / family contact evidence

### Takeaway
Large operators (Ryman) have deployed Wi-Fi across all villages, and residents increasingly arrive with smartphones. I found no NZ data on how common resident Wi-Fi or captive portals are in rest homes. COVID-19 lockdown research in NZ shows video calls became the main family link but often needed staff to set up and timeslot them. That is exactly the staff burden a one-button device could remove.

### Cited Findings
- Ryman rolled out Wi-Fi across all its villages as part of its digital care project (2019 coverage) — [HINZ](https://www.hinz.org.nz/news/457447/myRyman-electronic-care-system-deployed-to-3500-residents-rooms.htm); [Healthcare IT News](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/asia/ryman-healthcare-develops-own-electronic-care-system-its-aged-care-residents)
- A new IT and network solution was deployed for "NZ's largest retirement village group" (date in article) — [ECD Online case study](https://www.ecdonline.com.au/content/data-networking-communications/case-study/new-it-solution-for-nz-s-largest-retirement-village-group-852798348)
- NZ study of Chinese care-home residents' video calling (WeChat) in the 2020–21 lockdowns: calls made residents calmer and families less lonely, but some residents could not place calls without staff help, and staff had to set time limits so everyone got a turn — [Journal of Long-Term Care](https://journal.ilpnetwork.org/articles/10.31389/jltc.153)
- Australia/NZ family interview study: video calls were the main way to see relatives during lockdowns, used for social contact and wellbeing checks. Some saw them as a poor substitute, and some residents were unfamiliar with the technology — [JMIR / PMC10263180](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10263180)
- Qualitative NZ study of 44 older people: loneliness was experienced as disconnection and worsened by stay-at-home rules — [Aging & Mental Health / Tandfonline, 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13607863.2023.2211549); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37178140)
- An NZ participant went two months without visiting a spouse with dementia in a rest home, with contact limited to staff-arranged video links a few times a week — [PMC8646691](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8646691/)
- Christchurch-developed tech helped village residents stay healthy during the pandemic (2020) — [1News, Aug 2020](https://www.1news.co.nz/2020/08/20/christchurch-developed-technology-helps-retirement-village-residents-keep-healthy-amid-pandemic/)

### Inferences
- Staff-mediated video calling was the COVID default and consumed staff time. A resident-operated, always-on, one-button radio directly addresses the "residents couldn't place calls themselves" finding.
- **Captive portals are the main technical risk:** guest Wi-Fi in hospitality-style village centres often uses splash pages. The sales pitch should include a pre-sales checklist (an SSID without a captive portal, or a MAC-allowlist/IoT SSID) and a "works on your existing resident Wi-Fi, outbound HTTPS only" message for operator IT teams.

### Gaps
- I found no survey of resident Wi-Fi availability or captive-portal prevalence in NZ rest homes and villages, and no NZ virtual-visiting programme data (number of facilities or devices).

## 6. Workforce: staff numbers and shortages

### Takeaway
ARC depends heavily on migrants: about 45% of workers, 59% of RNs and 51% of healthcare assistants are on visas. Aged care is modelled to be about 1,550 RNs short of safe staffing (Aug 2025), and a third of facilities reported nurse vacancies (2023). Tools that cut staff time on non-clinical requests and on arranging family contact speak to a real pain point.

### Cited Findings
- About 45% of residential care workers are on visas, including 59% of RNs and 51% of healthcare assistants (ACA estimate) — [NZ Doctor, May 2026](https://www.nzdoctor.co.nz/article/undoctored/aged-care-association-calls-urgent-national-workforce-plan-following-latest); [Scoop, May 2026](https://community.scoop.co.nz/2026/05/aged-care-association-calls-for-urgent-national-workforce-plan-following-latest-immigration-changes/)
- In the 2023 ACA member survey, 33% of respondents had nurse vacancies at the time (the latest found; dated) — [Search summary of ACA material](https://www.nzdoctor.co.nz/article/undoctored/aged-care-association-calls-urgent-national-workforce-plan-following-latest) (verify)
- Infometrics for NZNO (Aug 2025): aged care is short about 1,550 RNs to meet safe staffing levels — [Infometrics/NZNO, Aug 2025](https://www.nzno.org.nz/Portals/0/publications/Report%20-%20Infometrics%20report%20how%20many%20more%20nurses%20does%20New%20Zealand%20need%20August%202025%20FINAL.pdf)
- Workforce data: about 16,300 personal-care assistants and carers for the aged/disabled against ~2,100 RNs, roughly 8 care workers per RN. **Flag:** the 2,100 RN figure seems low for ARC nationally, so check the occupation definition — [MWT Bulletin](https://mwtbulletin.com/job-placement/new-zealands-aged-care-crisis-is-more-than-a-nursing-shortage/) (secondary source, verify)
- Oceania alone employs 2,200 staff (FY26) — [Oceania FY26 Annual Report](https://api.oceaniahealthcare.co.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/FY26-Annual-Report.pdf)
- May 2026: the ACA says NZ has no publicly visible long-term ARC workforce plan. Oct 2026: it criticised restrictions on 2027 nursing student enrolments — [NZ Doctor, May 2026](https://www.nzdoctor.co.nz/article/undoctored/aged-care-association-calls-urgent-national-workforce-plan-following-latest); [Scoop Business, 14 Oct 2026](https://business.scoop.co.nz/2026/10/14/health-nz-is-not-the-whole-health-system-nursing-training-decision-exposes-workforce-planning-failure/)
- Nursing students are rejecting aged-care careers (Aug 2026) — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/nursing-students-are-rejecting-careers-in-aged-care-a-staffing-crisis-is-coming-290137)

### Inferences
- A "talk to the nurse station" knob position could deflect walk-to-room checks and call-bell presses for non-urgent needs. A pilot could measure it as staff minutes saved per resident per day. The staff-time argument is likely stronger with operators than the loneliness argument alone.
- Many care staff speak English as a second language (high visa share), so push-to-talk voice should be clear, and staff-side handsets or apps should be simple.

### Gaps
- I found no total ARC headcount (all roles) for 2025–26 and no ACA vacancy survey newer than 2023.
