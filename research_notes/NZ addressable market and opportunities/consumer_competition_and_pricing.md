# NZ Consumer (B2C) Market: Competition, Pricing, Adoption Benchmarks and Channels for "Grandad's radio" and a kids' mini phone

Research date: 15 November 2026. Prices are NZD incl. GST unless stated. Method note: the research environment blocked direct fetches of msd.govt.nz, www.msd.govt.nz, workandincome.govt.nz, announcements.asx.com.au and tincan.kids (DNS not allowed). Figures for those sources come from search-engine extracts of the pages and are marked [verify]. Retail prices come from search extracts of retailer listings seen in November 2026. Listings change often, so recheck them before using any figure in a pitch.

## 1. Competing and substitute products in NZ, with NZ prices

### Takeaway
Nobody sells a one-button Wi-Fi push-to-talk device for seniors in NZ. The substitutes split into price bands:
- Simple senior phones: about NZ$109-199 each, and they need a mobile plan.
- Kids' 4G smartwatches: NZ$189-285, plus about NZ$17/month (app plus SIM).
- Smart displays: about NZ$399.
- Toy-grade UHF CB walkie-talkie twin packs: NZ$80-180.

The most direct competitor to the planned kids' version is Tin Can, which opened NZ pre-orders at NZ$190 in 2026 and ships by 30 November 2026. A retail price of NZ$100-250 per pair sits between toy UHF radios and a single senior phone or smartwatch.

### Inferences
- The product's price window is set by its substitutes:
  - Floor: toy UHF twin packs at NZ$80-90. They offer no range or app.
  - Mid: one senior phone at NZ$109-199 plus a plan; one Tin Can at NZ$190.
  - Ceiling: one Echo Show 8 at NZ$399, or a Spacetalk at NZ$189-285 plus about NZ$200/year.

  A pair at NZ$199-249 with no subscription is defensible against these. "No monthly fee" is a real differentiator against Spacetalk (about NZ$17/month) and Kitcal (subscription).
- Tin Can reaching NZ in November 2026 shows parents in NZ and Australia will pay about NZ$190 per unit for a screen-free, approved-contacts device. It also means the kids' version would arrive second.
- At NZ$35-50 parts cost per radio, a pair costs NZ$70-100 in parts. At NZ$199-249 retail with a 17-18% retailer gross margin (section 6), little would be left after freight, packaging, support and server costs. Direct-to-consumer sales improve the economics.

## 2. Personal medical alarms as the closest adoption benchmark among older NZers

### Takeaway
Medical alarms are the clearest proof that older NZers, their families and the state pay for a one-button device. In 2021 MSD funded more than 50,000 alarm clients through the Disability Allowance, and St John held about 62% of them. Pricing runs about NZ$16-26/week, or roughly NZ$850-1,350/year, and suppliers raised prices from 29 June 2026. MSD-funded users alone equal roughly 5-6% of NZ's about 900,000 people aged 65+. Total users including private payers are higher, but no current total was found.

### Inferences
- Penetration: 50,000+ MSD-funded alarms against about 900,000 aged 65+ is about 5.5% (2021 numerator, 2025 denominator). Most alarm users are 75+. The 80-84 band was not found; assuming about 130,000 for it (unsourced), 75+ is about 415,000, so funded alarms alone equal about 12% of that group. Private payers would push the real figure higher. This suggests "one-button device for an older person" can reach low double-digit penetration of 75+ when it is subsidised and safety-framed.
- Social connection is a "want" rather than a safety "need", and the product has no MSD subsidy. Realistic consumer adoption should be planned well below alarm penetration, say 0.5-2% of 75+ households over several years (a judgement, not sourced). That is roughly 2,000-8,000 elderly users.
- Alarm pricing (about NZ$900+/year, every year) sets a high reference point. A one-off NZ$200-250 radio pair with no monthly fee is cheap next to it. It also suggests a combined "talk + SOS" positioning could support a higher price, but that would bring the product into accredited-supplier and monitoring territory.

## 3. Kids: Spacetalk / smartwatch adoption, first-phone age, child numbers, school phone ban

### Takeaway
NZ children usually get their own phone between ages 8 and 12. About a quarter have one by Year 4 (about age 8), 64% by Year 7 (about age 11) and over 90% by high school. A 2degrees/Netsafe parent survey puts the typical first phone at age 11-12. That leaves a pre-phone window of roughly ages 5-10. The school phone ban has applied since April 2024, and an ERO review (November 2025) found it working and raised whether smartwatches should be covered too. Spacetalk has NZ retail presence and about 60,600 mobile subscribers group-wide (mostly Australian). No NZ subscriber breakdown was found.

### Cited Findings

## 4. Smartphone ownership and video calling among older NZers by age band

### Takeaway
No current (2024-2026) NZ source was found that breaks smartphone ownership or video calling out for ages 75+. Older data and over-50s surveys suggest the 75+ group lags well behind: 23% of 75+ owned a smartphone around 2014, and 34% of over-50s cited video calls as a reason to go online in 2022. This is a weak area.

### Cited Findings
- About 2014: smartphone ownership was 47.6% of those aged 65-74 and 23% of those aged 75+ (dated). — [Scoop](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/BU1411/S00631/nearly-80-of-nz-adults-will-have-a-smartphone-within-a-year.htm)
- A Spark survey of over-55s (undated) found 68% own a smartphone, 62% a tablet, and 88% use the internet several times a day. — [GrownUps](https://grownups.co.nz/?p=1526666)
- The NZ Seniors Digital Trends Report 2022 (1,129 respondents aged 50+) found 34% cited video calls or conferencing with family and friends as a motivation for going online. — [NZ Seniors](https://www.nzseniors.co.nz/news-insights/new-zealand-seniors-series-digital-trends-report-2022)
- The InternetNZ Internet Insights 2025 survey (1,003 respondents, online) excludes people who are offline, so it is not representative of the oldest group. — [InternetNZ 2025](https://internetnz.nz/assets/Archives/New-Zealands-Internet-Insights-2025-survey-findings.pdf)

### Inferences
- The target user (85+, or 75+ with low digital confidence) is likely the group where smartphone ownership and confident video-call use are lowest. The trend in younger senior cohorts, however, means the "phone is too hard" segment shrinks over time.

### Gaps
- Stats NZ Household Use of ICT and Digital Inclusion data by 75+/85+ band were not retrieved.
- Video-calling frequency for 75+ was not found.

## 6. Market-size benchmarks, retailer margins and crowdfunding precedents

### Takeaway
Market estimates are broad and vary widely by research firm:
- Kids' smartwatches: about US$1.2-10.7b globally in 2025.

### Inferences
- With a 17-18% blended chain gross margin, a NZ$229 retail pair yields about NZ$190 wholesale. Niche, slow-moving devices typically need a higher retailer margin (often 25-40%) to win shelf space (industry norm, unsourced). That would leave about NZ$140-170 for a pair costing NZ$70-100 in parts. This supports launching direct-to-consumer first, with retail later.
- Global market reports are not useful for sizing NZ. Bottom-up NZ figures are better: about 415,000 aged 75+, about 100,000 aged 85+, about 520,000 children aged 5-12, and medical-alarm and Spacetalk penetration as calibration.
