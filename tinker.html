For tinkerers

The radio his younger self would have opened

Pop spent his working life making things go. So his radio is simple on the outside, with one big button, and open on the inside. Take the back off and there are sockets waiting, with no soldering.

Nothing is built yet. This is the design, decided on 16 November 2026. Prices are suggestions. Looking for the simple radio? It's on the front page.

Open it up

Four screws hold the back panel. Behind it the boards are labelled, there is room to spare, and nothing you plug in needs a soldering iron.

A base, and cards

Every radio has Talk, FM, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth for keyboards. The mesh radio and 4G are cards you add, when you want them.

Room to work

The case is 220 × 125 × 80 mm, far bigger than the boards need. There are screw points inside for whatever you add.

Pop never sees it

The sockets are inside. On the outside there is one new thing: a second USB-C port.

Pictures of the inside are still to be made.

The main chip is an ESP32-S3. A pin expander on the board keeps pins free for you. The socket page will be published for anyone to make cards from.

The first cards

Two from us to begin with, and one sensor that moved off the board.

4G

A modem and a SIM holder, for homes without Wi-Fi. A bigger antenna screws on outside for weak coverage, and the radio can share its data as a Wi-Fi hotspot. 5G can follow in the same bay.

A few dollars
TILT

The sensor that knows the radio is lying on its back for games. It plugs into a sensor socket, like any other.

Suggested prices. A built radio comes with the mesh card fitted, so every radio a family buys is a link in the mesh.

What it can do, and what it can't

Better to know before you take the back off.

It can

  • Take a keyboard. USB in the Data port, or a Bluetooth Low Energy one. Type a message on the radio's own screen and send it over the mesh.
  • Use any screen in the house. The radio serves a page on your Wi-Fi. Open it on a phone, a tablet, a laptop or a TV.
  • Cast. Send internet radio to a Chromecast or a Google TV from the knobs. Planned second, after the page.
  • Be a hotspot. With the 4G card, it shares mobile data over Wi-Fi: enough for messages, email and Wi-Fi calling from a phone with no signal. Not for video.
  • Run your code. Arduino, ESP-IDF, MicroPython or CircuitPython. The web flasher puts the official firmware back.

It can't

  • Drive a USB-C screen. The chip has no video output.
  • Play through Bluetooth speakers, headphones or hearing aids. It has Bluetooth Low Energy only. A card may fix that later.
  • Pair with older Bluetooth keyboards. Low Energy ones only. USB always works.
  • Do 5G. Not yet: the modems cost three to four times a 4G one.
  • Be a cell tower. Rebroadcasting mobile signal needs the network's approval. The hotspot is Wi-Fi only.
  • Carry voice over the mesh. Text only.

None of this is tested yet. The keyboard, the page and casting get tried on a dev board before the board is drawn.

What stays fixed

An open radio still has promises to keep, so the important ones are kept in hardware.

The red light can't be switched off

It is wired to the microphone's power. If the microphone can hear, the light is on, whatever firmware is running.

Pop's radio is locked. Yours isn't.

A built radio only runs firmware we signed. A kit runs anything. The family's phones show which radios are on official firmware.

Open to add to, not open to copy

Anyone may make and sell a card. The radio's own design and firmware stay free for non-commercial use, with a licence for the rest.

The kids' mini phone has no sockets and no mesh. A radio module you fit yourself may need its own approval; we are asking the regulator before any card is sold.
