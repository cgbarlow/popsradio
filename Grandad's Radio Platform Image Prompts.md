# Grandad's Radio Platform Image Prompts

Nov 15, 2026 · @Chris Barlow

Seven prompts for an image generator, for the platform unit in [Grandad's Radio as a Platform](Grandad's%20Radio%20as%20a%20Platform.md): the bedside scene redone with the new radio, a blueprint, a chess clock in a rest home, Pong from the player's seat, FM radio on the screen, the Talk list on the screen, and the radio carried with its strap over a shoulder.

## How to use

Paste one prompt at a time. Each is self-contained, so the generator needs no other context.

- **Consistency.** Every prompt repeats the same description of the radio (the paragraph that starts "A stout rectangular tabletop radio"), so the results look like one product. Change it in all seven if you change it in one.
- **The shape.** The radio is a stout rectangle, wider than it is tall: about 200 mm wide, 130 mm tall and 80 mm deep, so it sits low and is hard to knock over. If a picture comes out tall, square or thin, add "landscape shape, one and a half times as wide as it is tall". The case has straight sides down to the table, with no flared base; if a plinth or foot appears, add "the bottom edge is the same as the top edge".
- **The strap.** A long, narrow, adjustable shoulder strap about 20 mm wide, with a slide buckle, not a short handle and not a wide bag strap. Generators default to a short handle arching over the top. On a table, the prompts lay the spare length in loose loops beside the radio so its length shows.
- **The switch is set to PUSH.** The switch is small: a slot about 20 mm long, half the width of the orange button, with a thumb about 8 mm long hard against the left end, behind the word PUSH, and an empty dark groove to its right. Two ways it goes wrong: a thumb as long as half the slot looks halfway, so if that happens add "the thumb touches the left end of the slot"; and the words "long slot" make the switch stretch across the top, so keep it described as short and compact.
- **The orange button is the biggest thing on top, and dead centre.** It is about 40 mm wide and domed, midway between the left and right edges, with the switch on its left and the volume knob on its right. If it drifts to one side, add "the orange button lines up with the middle of the screen below it". The volume knob beside it is small, about 25 mm. Generators tend to make the knob big and the button small; if that happens, add "the orange button is twice the size of the volume knob".
- **The knobs.** Three black ribbed knobs: a small volume knob on top to the right of the orange button, and a big one sticking out of each side. Generators like to add more knobs or put them on the front. If one does, add "only three knobs in total" to the end.
- **One light.** One green status light, to the left of the screen. Generators like to add a matching one on the other side.
- **On its back.** In prompts 3 and 4 the radio lies on its back with the screen facing up. The top edge, with the orange button, points away from the player, and the speaker grille is nearest them.
- **Faces.** Prompts 3, 4 and 7 keep faces out of frame. If the generator adds one anyway, add "cropped at the chest, no faces visible".
- **The mode bar.** Upright screens show all four modes as tabs across the top (TALK, FM, NET, PLAY), with the current one highlighted, and the choices for that mode below. The chess clock and Pong use the whole screen, with no tabs. If the tabs come out garbled, cut the prompt back to "a row of four small tabs across the top, the first one highlighted".
- **Screen text.** Each prompt spells out every word on the screen and ends with "no other text on the screen", because generators fill an empty screen with made-up station names. If the words come out wrong, cut the small text first.
- **No antenna.** The FM antenna is a wire hidden inside the strap, so the prompts say there is none. Generators love adding one.
- **Aspect ratio.** 4:5 for most, 16:9 for the blueprint, 3:2 for the chess game. Change the last line if your tool sets this elsewhere.
